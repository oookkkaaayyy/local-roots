## 2. Layering vs blending: the physics
Model dyed plastic as an absorbing (almost non-scattering) skin over a scattering substrate. With negligible scatter in the dyed zone (KM limit S->0 [textbook]; Beer law for transparent layers, EPFL https://lspwww.epfl.ch/publications/colour/cmfpmraticr_06.pdf):
  R(lam) = Rg(lam) * exp(-2 * sum_i eps_i(lam) * N_i / cos_eff),  N_i = integral c_i(z) dz  (depth-integrated amount).
So absorbance is additive and order independent; the existing additive-baths model is the correct first-order law. Sequential vs simultaneous differs only through:
1. Amount N_i uptake: with Fickian uptake from constant surface concentration, c(x,t)=Cs*erfc(x/(2 sqrt(Dt))), N=Cs*2*sqrt(Dt/pi) [textbook]. Second dip does NOT see a fresh plastic: occupied free volume lowers its N (competitive sorption). Model: N_i = N_i,single * (1 - theta_other) with theta = sum_j N_j/N_sat (Langmuir-type) [prior]; fit N_sat per plastic.
2. Depth profile matters only if dyed zone scatters or dye sits below a scatterer (opaque/white plastic: surface-only dye vs through-dyed differ by factor ~2 path in absorbance if substrate is behind).
3. Interaction/aggregation: lambda_max shifts, not additive. Flag as evidence, don't model.
4. Thick scattering dyed zone: use finite-layer KM, R = (1-Rg(a-b coth bSh))/(a+b coth bSh-Rg), a=(K+S)/S, b=sqrt(a^2-1) [src https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.04884]; layers compose as 2x2 transfer matrices, gamma=sqrt(K(K+2S)) [src https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09139, EPFL Hebert/Hersch]. Single-constant mixing (K/S)_mix=(K/S)_sub+sum c_i[(K/S)_i - (K/S)_sub] holds only when a white substrate dominates scatter [src arXiv 2104.04884; spectral.js].
Surface correction (Saunderson), n=1.5: R_meas = rs+(1-rs)(1-ri)R/(1-ri R), rs=0.04 [textbook], ri=0.6 diffuse [src EPFL: r10=0.6, r01=0.1]. Needed when comparing to photos with specular or a clear skin; plastic with gloss has ~4% white veil that desaturates deep colors.

## 3. Plastic-aware absorption
- Existing Champion trick (base spectrum^0.38 after 0.08 floor) equals scaling absorbance by 0.38: physically consistent with Beer-Lambert (T^x = exp(-xA)). Generalize: T_thin = T_base^(d/d0), d/d0 = path ratio, per plastic.
- Plastic optical params [textbook/prior]: n(PP)=1.49, LDPE 1.51, HDPE 1.54, TPU ~1.50; neat polyolefin visible absorption ~0, UV cutoff ~300 nm; translucent grades scatter from crystallites (S0 ~0.2-1.5 /mm, S ~ (lam/550)^-p, p 0-1.5); white TiO2 grades S ~5-20 /mm. FIT per plastic.
- Uptake kinetics: apparent Arrhenius K=K0 exp(-E/RT) and Fick D from polyester disperse-dye papers: Ea 37.8-59.3 kcal/mol (158-248 kJ/mol), 83-100 C, Tg ~80 C [src https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6414876]; exhaustion at 120 min: 83C 50-58%, 90C 62-72%, 95C 71-80%, 100C 81-88%; uptake K/S vs t at 100C: 3.5, 9.3, 13.2, 14.9, 18.4, 19.3, 21.4 at 5,10,30,50,70,90,105 min. D (x1e-9 cm2/min; r not reported, use ratios) for Disperse Red 60/167 on PET/PCP/spandex 100-130 C in science_params.json [src https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12430684]: roughly x10 to x60 from 100 to 130 C, near-Tg step functions. Hill: D=0.06292 r^2/t_half.
- Recommended prior for disc plastics: Ea 60-250 kJ/mol, wide; fit from >=2 temperatures. Rule of thumb [prior]: dye depth sqrt(Dt), so 4x time doubles depth; absorbance N ~ sqrt(t) until surface/bath depletion, then plateau (K/S series above saturates ~ exhaustion).
- Rate-limiting kinetics (Eq. 3 PMC6414876): K_T = X/(1-X)^2 + 0.5 ln[(1-X)/(1+X)], X=uptake fraction; K/S=(1-R)^2/(2R) is used as strength measure (linear in conc. only below ~K/S 10-20; beyond that saturates).

## 4. Fluorescent and UV dyes
Add emission as a separate non-subtractive term (radiance factor may exceed 1): Day-Glo Alberta Yellow (coumarin) has 80% base reflectance and peak radiance factor 177% at 500-550 nm; dye cascades (Alberta + rhodamine F5G) exceed 300% [src https://osa.magnet.fsu.edu/teachersparents/articles/pdfs/fluorescence and the color of day glo paints.pdf].
  L(lam_em) = R_refl(lam_em) + sum_ex QY * [1 - 10^(-A(lam_ex))] * I(lam_ex)/(I norm) * phi(lam_em)  (photon basis; energy factor lam_ex/lam_em), A = eps*c*d total absorbance at lam_ex, phi area-normalized emission. Use only the fluorescent dye's share of absorbance (inner filter: other dyes absorbing at lam_ex/lam_em reduce output).
Per-dye QY (ethanol, [src PhotochemCAD pages]): fluorescein basic 0.97 (neutral 0.79), rhodamine 6G 0.95, rhodamine 123 0.90, sulforhodamine 101 0.90, rhodamine B 0.70 (0.49 in water literature; Kellogg 0.79 listed on page 010 is fluorescein), eosin Y 0.67, rose bengal 0.11, coumarin 1 0.73, coumarin 314 0.68, coumarin 343 0.63, coumarin 30 0.67, DCM 0.43-0.60, Nile red 0.70, perylene diimide 0.97, quinine sulfate 0.546, acridine orange 0.20, proflavin 0.34, DAPI 0.043 (free). Peaks (abs/em nm): fluorescein 500/540 (basic), R6G 530/552, RhB 545/565, eosin 525/543, C1 373/446, C343 445/462, C314 436/476, C30 407/478.
UV 365 and 395 nm: use eps_365, eps_395 columns in science_params.json. Strong UV absorbers (coumarin 1, POPOP, DPH, quinine, DAPI) give blue 430-510 under blacklight; xanthenes absorb little at 365 (so apparent UV brightness needs a UV sensitizer; 395 LEDs excite more via 400-420 tail) [photochemcad data]. Solid-matrix effects (QY drop at high loading, 5-20 nm red shift, reabsorption/self-quench) [textbook/prior].
Glow (SrAl2O4:Eu,Dy) [src https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1376499]: emission peak 520 nm, FWHM 83 nm (450-650), excitation 300-450 nm (best ~365-370), optimum 6 mol% Eu / 1 mol% Dy / 20 mol% H3BO3; after 10 min of 365 nm, three-exponential decay tau = 11.7, 53.3, 255 s. Model I(t)=sum a_i e^(-t/tau_i) [src], with long-tail power law for eye-visible hours [textbook]. Charge fraction scales with 300-450 nm dose.

## 5. Perceptual fit
CIEDE2000: use Sharma-Wu-Dalal test pairs https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/gsharma/ciede2000/dataNprograms/ciede2000testdata.txt and notes https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/gsharma/ciede2000/ciede2000noteCRNA.pdf to unit-test the engine's dE00 (hue-branch for |h1-h2|>180, h'=0 when C'=0). Practical: JND ~1, side-by-side visible 2-3 [textbook]. Existing measured anchors (mean dE00 6.15 Champion; photos 11.3) are dominated by photo/lighting error; weigh by direction tests.
